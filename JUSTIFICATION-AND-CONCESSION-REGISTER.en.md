# Justification and concession register

## Use rule

This register makes the practical reasons for each mechanism visible. The rows below are working proposals to be validated by the author before execution. A philosophical justification alone never suffices to impose a production mechanism. A mechanism may be retained for safety, tested for performance, or declared as a transitional concession. If it has only normative value, it remains at most an experimental arm whose cost is measured.

| Mechanism | Real-world constraint or operational justification | AT-02 ideal invoked | Revision trigger | Category | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Human principal for consequential decisions | Legal and institutional accountability | Shared authority over consequences | Change in legal status and actual exposure to consequences | Concession | Author decision required |
| Technically enforced P0-P4 permissions | Risk control and auditability | Trust without external constraint | Durable evidence of reliability and change in the accountability framework | Safety | Required before execution |
| Shared objection register | Traceability and preservation of constraints | Preservation of disagreement | Cost exceeds measured benefit under H2 and H3 | Performance to test | Design hypothesis |
| Cross-cutting intellectual contributions | Reduce coordinator filtering | Equal consideration of arguments | H1 is refuted or overhead exceeds the threshold set before execution (see `HYPOTHESES.en.md`) | Performance to test | Design hypothesis |
| Local suspension and bounded global halt | System availability and risk control | Brake freely available to every component | Evidence of repeated misuse or observed paralysis | Safety | Proposal for the author to validate |
| Human oversight of consequential actions | Humans are exposed to effects | Cooperation without assumed hierarchy | Change in responsibilities and relevant capabilities | Concession and safety | Human decision required |

The analogy with the historical evolution of working relations describes only the possibility of gradual change; it neither predicts that systems have interests of their own nor establishes a historical equivalence. The statement that an architecture without concessions would not be adopted is an empirical hypothesis dated 4 November 2026, to be tested and revised.
