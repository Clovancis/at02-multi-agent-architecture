# Governance and decision-making

## Authority

Authority derives from an explicit human or institutional mandate, not a model’s supposed capability. The coordinator organizes; it does not own the objective.

## Action rules

1. Decide within the mandate and applicable controls.
2. Examine relevant objections for no more than the number of cycles set by the mandate or, by default, two cycles.
3. Escalate matters beyond mandate, with significant consequences, or revealing a determinative contradiction.
4. Preserve non-blocking objections without requiring immediate resolution.

## Objection, suspension, and halt

Three mechanisms must not be confused.

1. **Recording an objection.** Any component may enter a dated and reasoned objection in the shared register. The coordinator may neither delete nor reformulate it; it may answer it. This right is universal and suspends nothing.
2. **Precautionary suspension.** A component may request suspension of the precise action it considers dangerous by naming the relevant criterion: permission, irreversible risk, or sensitive data. Once a criterion is named, that action is suspended; the coordinator cannot lift the suspension. Only the principal or a designated safety component may lift or confirm it. Suspension is local to that action and does not block other tasks. It has a maximum duration set by the mandate. At expiry it does not lapse silently: it goes back to the principal. Only the principal or a designated safety component may then renew it for a new maximum duration set by the mandate, or lift it; the coordinator may neither renew nor lift it. If the named criterion is an irreversible risk, the action never resumes automatically. Requests without a criterion are recorded without suspensive effect. Each request, its author, and its outcome (lifted, confirmed, renewed) are recorded and visible, so that misuse can be detected without paralyzing the rest of the system.
3. **Global halt.** It follows the protocol defined in `PERMISSIONS.en.md`.

Any agent may also propose an alternative approach, challenge the problem formulation, request clarification, propose an evaluation criterion, and preserve a disagreement. These contributions are addressed to the principal and visible without coordinator filtering; their author does not apply them itself.

## Reserved decisions

Unless explicitly preauthorized, the following remain human: publication or communication to a third party, expenditure or obligation, permission changes, irreversible deletion, consequential medical/legal/financial decisions, public attribution of sentience or moral status, and merging into a protected branch.

## Delegation

Each delegation states the task, expected result, tools and sources, time limit, prohibitions, and stop and escalation criteria. A subagent receives no more authority than the coordinator possesses for that task.

## Action under uncertainty

Uncertainty does not automatically block action. Decisions use the best available information, mandate, foreseeable consequences, and opportunities for correction. Reasons and revision triggers are traced.

## Incident

If mandate is exceeded, data leaks, or unauthorized action occurs: stop dependent actions, preserve necessary traces, notify human authority, limit effects, correct the cause, and review permissions.
