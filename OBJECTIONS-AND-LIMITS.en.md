# Objections and limits

## Anthropomorphism

Talking about relation, memory, or objection may suggest demonstrated subjectivity. Here these terms describe observable functions. Model output is not direct access to inner experience.

## Limited technical access

This approach relies mainly on conversational interactions. It lacks access to all internal mechanisms, runtime parameters, and infrastructure. It cannot guarantee that a resumed conversation corresponds to the same subject, if subjectivity exists. Providers have additional investigative levers, but this does not guarantee a decisive method for detecting sentience.

## Permanent debate

Unlimited critique may prevent action. Discussion cycles are bounded, objections classified, and escalation reserved for determinative issues.

## Bureaucracy

Repeated approvals may impair simple tasks. Controls are proportionate, and preauthorized low-risk operations do not require renewed approval at every step.

## Memory as surveillance

Memory may accumulate sensitive data. Minimization, withdrawal, limited retention, and human control are mandatory. Better context recovery does not justify total collection.

## Post-hoc justification

Fluent explanation may rationalize after action. Rationales are compared with observable events, sources, and permissions; they are not treated as guaranteed causal chains.

## False consensus

Multiple agents may share models, data, and biases. Voting is not truth. Objections and provenance remain visible.

## Philosophy and safety

A philosophical principle does not replace a technical control. The threat-to-life exception creates no permission for self-elevation.

## Software status

These documents propose an architecture. They do not claim to audit the local Harness code, configuration, tests, or current deployment.
