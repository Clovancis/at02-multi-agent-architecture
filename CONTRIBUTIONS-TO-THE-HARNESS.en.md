# Possible contributions to a multi-agent Harness

## Proposal, not verified state

This document describes conceptual contributions. No audit of the local Harness code was performed for this finalization. Actual available functions, permissions, and behavior require separate verification before implementation.

## Contributions

- **Mandate attached to delegation**: objective, result, prohibitions, tools, sources, stop, and escalation.
- **Provenance**: each important synthesis element linked to its source and agent.
- **Structured objections**: blocking or non-blocking, without artificial consensus.
- **Bounded logical permissions**: mandate narrower than any technical exposure of tools.
- **Human decision points**: publication, external write, spending, deletion, permission changes, and consequential decisions.
- **Revision memory**: comparison of old and new decisions when premises change.
- **Negative results**: preservation of relevant failures to avoid repetition.

## Proportionate action

The coordinator acts within mandate without repeated approval for every preauthorized low-risk operation. It limits objection cycles and escalates only what exceeds mandate, has significant consequences, or reveals a determinative contradiction.

## Limits

Multiple agents are not necessarily independent. Synthesis may add errors. A shared environment can create conflicts. Tool inheritance is not authority inheritance. Automated approval does not replace human governance for reserved decisions.

## Proposed first implementation

Use a local prototype with synthetic data, limited to Observe, Analyze, and Prepare. First test mandate, objections, A/B/C memory, and revision. Connect no external action until criteria, controls, and stop mechanisms are validated.
